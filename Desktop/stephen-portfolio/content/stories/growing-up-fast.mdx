I was twelve years old when I stopped being a child.

## Home

At home it was my mother, one of my sisters, and me, the last born.

We didn't have much, but we had each other. My mother held everything together the way mothers do.

Then she fell very sick. So sick that for a while we didn't know if she would survive.

When the person holding everything together can no longer stand, someone has to pick it up. At twelve, that someone was me.

## The hustle

So I started hustling.

Mostly mjengo, construction work. Carrying heavy loads, mixing, doing whatever the builders told me to do. And any other hard work I could find. Whatever paid something.

My body was a child's body doing a man's work. At the end of the day I was exhausted, and the little I earned went home: food, my mother's needs, whatever was most urgent that day.

School became something I squeezed in between the work, when I could.

## The road I almost took

I was young, tired, and around older people who lived hard lives. Slowly, I started picking up habits that were pulling me down.

I'm not proud of that time. But I won't pretend it didn't happen, because someone reading this might be on that same road right now.

I was a boy carrying adult problems, and I was coping in ways a child shouldn't have to cope. If nothing had changed, I don't know where I would be today.

But something did change.

## What those years gave me

I wouldn't wish those years on any child. But I can't deny what they taught me:

- **How to work hard.** Nothing I've done since has felt as heavy as mjengo at twelve.
- **How to carry responsibility.** Years later, when my sister's children needed someone, I already knew how to carry a family.
- **How to keep going.** When you've survived the worst days, the hard days don't scare you as much.

The boy who carried loads on building sites now builds software. The hands are the same. The work is different.

> I can't change the past, but I can control my future.

## For you

If you're a child hustling right now to help your family, I see you. I was you.

Here's what I want you to know:

- **It's not your fault.** You're doing something brave that you should never have had to do.
- **Hold on to school,** even if it's only a little at a time. It's the door out.
- **Stay away from the things that numb the pain.** They feel like help, but they will take everything from you.
- **Let someone in.** A teacher, a neighbour, someone at church. Tell them what's happening. Someone is watching, and someone wants to help.

Your story isn't finished at twelve. Mine wasn't.
