Her name was Mary. She was my sister, and she was my best sister.

## Who she was

Mary loved music. She loved to dance. And she loved God.

One of my favourite memories of her is a simple one. A few weeks after she had one of her children, we were staying together, and we danced. Just the two of us, music playing, laughing. Nothing special was happening that day. That's exactly why I remember it. It was a normal, happy day with my sister.

Sometimes I think my love of music started with her.

## The end

I was still in school when she got sick. I didn't understand much of what was happening. By the time we were together again, she was already in the last stages of her illness.

She was suffering. I won't describe it, because she deserves to be remembered for her dancing, not her pain.

But I will never forget that she was calling my name. Calling me for help. I was just a child, and there was nothing I could do.

And before she died, she told me something:

*Her children were my responsibility.*

I was too young to understand what that meant. I only understood it years later.

## The burial

We buried Mary.

That same day, I was introduced to my father for the first time. I had grown up without knowing him, and the day I met him was the day I said goodbye to her. It's strange how life puts the biggest moments side by side.

## What losing her taught me

Before Mary died, I believed the people I loved would always be there. That's what children believe.

Losing her changed how I see life. People are not here forever. Every day with someone you love is a gift, not a guarantee.

So I try to show people what they mean to me now, while they're here. And I try to make my life count, because I know how short it can be.

## The promise

Years later, Mary's children lost their father too, and ended up with nobody. By then I was in campus, still depending on myself. But I remembered her words.

Her children were my responsibility.

So I took them in. And today, they're doing well in school, and I'm proud of them every day. I hope Mary would be proud too.

## To Mary

I hope you are happy where you are. Your children are safe. I'm keeping my promise.

## For you

If you've just lost someone you love, I won't tell you it stops hurting. It doesn't, not completely. But I can tell you this:

> Life has to continue.

Not because you forget them, but because you carry them with you. Live in a way that honours them. Keep the promises they asked of you. And remember them for how they lived, not how they left.
