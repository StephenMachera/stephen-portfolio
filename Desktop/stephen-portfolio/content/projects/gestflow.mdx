title: "GestFlow"
---

Moving work from one computer to another is still clumsy. You upload it to the cloud,
email it to yourself, or reach for a USB stick.

GestFlow asks a simpler question: **what if you could just pick it up and throw it?**

Make a grab gesture in front of your laptop, and GestFlow captures what you're working
on — a file in VS Code, a page in your browser, a video in VLC. Throw your hand to the
right, and it appears on the other computer, open and ready, exactly where you left off.
No setup, no cables, no accounts.

## How it works

I built GestFlow in six phases, each one a working piece on its own.

**1. The gesture engine.** MediaPipe tracks 21 points on your hand through a normal
webcam. A TensorFlow model I trained reads those points and recognises **8 gestures**
— grab, open palm, pinch, throw right, throw left, expand, select multiple and cancel —
with **98.19% accuracy**. It runs as a lightweight TFLite model.

**2. The content grabber.** When you grab, GestFlow works out what's on your screen
and asks the right app for its state. Each app has its own adapter: a Chrome extension
for the browser, an extension for VS Code, and a window-based adapter for VLC.

**3. State capture.** Everything is packed into a small, validated packet — what the
content is, where you were in it, and which device it came from. Packets expire after
30 seconds, so nothing old gets thrown by accident.

**4. The transfer.** Devices on the same Wi-Fi find each other automatically using
mDNS — that's the "zero setup" part. Packets travel directly between computers over
WebSockets, peer-to-peer, with retries and acknowledgements so nothing goes missing.

**5. The receiver.** On the other side, GestFlow opens the right app and puts your
work back in place.

**6. Putting it together.** The camera runs silently in the background, and a desktop
notification confirms each grab and each throw.

## A problem worth mentioning

Early on, devices kept "forgetting" each other. A peer that went quiet for 30 seconds
was treated as gone — far too aggressive for real Wi-Fi. The fix was to give peers
more patience (120 seconds), have every device re-announce itself every 20 seconds,
and send a small keep-alive ping every 15 seconds. Small numbers, big difference.

## Where it is now

All six phases are complete and working end to end, tested between an Ubuntu laptop
and a Windows 11 machine on the same network. What's left before version 1 is polish:
clearer error messages, a simple setup script, and a demo video.
